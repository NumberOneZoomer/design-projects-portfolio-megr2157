#About Ezra K. Bono
Hello I am Ezra Bono, a student at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte where I am currently in my second year of mechanical engineering. Growing up my brain was always full of questions as I wondered how things work and their design, through my curiosity I would go online and watch videos, read books, or simply ask people how do certain things work or operate. This would ironically leave me with more questions than answers as I would push to learn more and more. Growing older, a large portion of my friends and family were actually engineers of some kind, regardless of their discipline they all helped pushed me onto this path of becoming a engineer. Due to some financial, and personal reasons I was unable to pursue a bachelors in nuclear engineering but instead in mechanical engineering. While I do quite enjoy mechanical engineering my passion is still heavily rooted with energy and sustainable energy production which is why I plan on getting a concentration in energy and further pursing my education with a masters in nuclear engineering. But during my time here at UNCC studying mechanical engineering has vastly expanded my skillset and interdisciplinary studies and communication allowing me to work much more smoothly with others. It is thanks to my time spent with broad ranges of people and experiences that being an engineer is much more than just solving a problem, rather you must be able to communicate, explain and prove your solution works as well, something I am still hard at work learning to do to this day.

I personally believe that defending an engineering decision is the ability to properly communicate your thought process, decision making and solutions in a clear, concise and easy to understand manner. You must be able to explain all the intricate details to other engineers while also being able to word it in a way such that someone without the technical background, such as engineering, can understand it as well. Without the ability to defend your decisions with evidence or proof, there is no trust that the solution you came up with is the best/right one. Even if YOU know it's the best solution you must also be able to communicate that with your peers. I currently defend my decisions with evidence that support it, evidence that disproves/ goes against other solutions (such as higher inefficiently), and any other factors that may play a role such as financial, time or material constraints. I also understand sometimes I could be wrong and I must be able to admit when I am wrong have to change my thought process to achieve a better outcome.
